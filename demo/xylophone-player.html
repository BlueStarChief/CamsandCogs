Xylophone Player Cams

Two cams on one shaft play the tune. The turn cam pushes a gear rack that turns his body to each bar. The strike cam is a snail cam that lifts the mallet, holds it up while he turns, then drops it. The notes you hear come from the mechanism itself: wherever the mallet lands, that bar plays.

Every check samples the cams every 0.05° (7,200 points per turn). Hits are found by watching the mallet come down, not read from the tune.

    Tune and mechanism

    Each beat goes: strike (mallet drops onto the bar), lift (mallet rises), then turn to the next bar, as long as the mallet is high enough. The xylophone is the Eight-Bar design: C6–C7 aluminium bars on a 135 mm arc.

    Parts to make

    The SVG holds both cams and the pinion at true millimetre size: red lines are cuts, blue is engraving. Both cams share the shaft, so line up their engraved start marks with each other and point them at the followers. The turn cam's accuracy matters most: see “Land within” above.
